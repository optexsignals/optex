Investment process

Two long-only stock strategies decide what the portfolio owns. A futures overlay decides how much market risk it carries.

Volatility-managed trend

Core

Core trades large-cap US stocks. Each stock's short-term volatility is ranked against its own past year. Core buys strong performers when a high-volatility spell gives way to a quiet one, and sells when volatility returns to the stock, which usually happens as the advance rolls over.

Positions are equal-weighted at 1.5% of capital, up to 50 names, with no rebalancing while held. There is no sector model; diversification comes from the number of names.

Its market beta is about 0.4, with negative loadings on value and profitability. On top of its factor exposures it adds about 6% a year of alpha.

Short-term mean reversion

Contrarian

Contrarian looks for capitulation in Nasdaq-100 members: a run of heavy selling that leaves a stock deeply oversold at a time when its volatility is already elevated. These are the conditions in which forced and emotional sellers pay for liquidity.

It enters in tranches of 3.3% of capital, adding up to three times (10%) if the selling continues, and exits on the rebound, usually within days. It is in cash most of the time and busiest in market-wide panics, when Core is cutting back.

Its returns load on the short-term reversal factor, with little market beta.

Market timing

Compass

Compass is a daily market-state model built on breadth, volatility, sentiment and trend inputs. It produces a directional reading for the Nasdaq-100 each day.

When the reading is not decisive, which over 2000–2026 was a little under half of all days, Compass sizes a short Nasdaq-100 futures position to offset the stock strategies' exposure, leaving the portfolio close to market-neutral. When the reading is decisive, Compass takes the whole portfolio net long or net short, up to 100% either way. Position size is scaled down when realized volatility is high.

Most of the portfolio's alpha comes from Compass. Its edge is at the daily horizon, and static factor loadings explain only about a quarter of its weekly return variance.

Portfolio construction

How the three are combined

Capital goes to the stock strategies in a fixed order, Compass sets net exposure on top, and every limit is checked before the portfolio is published.

First call on capital

Contrarian

Contrarian has priority. If its entries need more cash than is free, Core sells its oldest positions to make room, and buys them back later if they still qualify.

Remaining capital

Core

Core invests what is left, 1.5% per name. Together the two stock strategies never exceed 100% of capital, so stocks are never held on margin.

Net exposure

Compass

Compass measures the stock strategies' exposure and trades Nasdaq-100 futures to the target net position. It only trades when the position has drifted meaningfully from target, which cuts turnover with little effect on returns.

Limitations

Where it can go wrong

Persistent melt-ups

Compass treats extreme readings as risk. In a long, euphoric rally it can sit hedged or short for months while the index keeps rising. Tests on the late-1990s bubble, before the published record starts, show this costing the portfolio for several years.

A short later period

The post-2021 data covers under five years, and some design choices were made after it was first seen. The live record only starts in November 2026.

Daily-horizon edge

Compass's edge is at the one-day horizon and assumes fills at the official open. Late execution, missed opens or wider spreads at the open erode it faster than they erode the stock strategies.

One index, one sector tilt

All stock positions are Nasdaq-100 members and the hedge is Nasdaq-100 futures, so the portfolio carries the index's technology concentration whenever it is net long.

Futures sizing

Below about $250,000, whole MNQ contracts cannot match the target futures position closely, and tracking error against the published portfolio rises.

One model carries the alpha

Compass supplies about half of the factor-adjusted alpha over 2000–2021 and almost all of it since 2022. If its signal stopped working, most of the edge would go with it, and the stock strategies alone would deliver little more than their market exposure.

Gap risk

Gross exposure can reach 300%. With the futures fully short and the stock strategies fully invested, a large overnight rally loses on the futures while the stocks only partly offset it.

Daily process

From the close to the open

Step 1

The close

After the US close the models run on final end-of-day data: Nasdaq-100 membership, prices, breadth and sentiment. Each strategy updates its entry and exit signals.

Step 2

The portfolio

Trades are combined in the fixed order, Contrarian, then Core, then the Compass futures target, and every position and exposure limit is checked.

Step 3

Publication

The portfolio for the next session, with every order for the open, is published on this site, emailed to subscribers and committed to the public Git repository.

Step 4

The open

Every order executes at the next open. Nothing changes during the session.
