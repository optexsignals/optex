The approach

Low beta, by construction

Compass hedges the stock books' market exposure on most days, so beta to the S&P 500 has averaged about 0.3. The book was positive through five of the six major S&P 500 sell-offs since 2008. The exception was the 2011 debt-ceiling sell-off.

Alpha after factor controls

Weekly alpha against the Fama-French five factors, momentum, short-term reversal and betting-against-beta is shown on the performance page.

No discretion

Signals are computed from end-of-day data after the close, and every trade executes at the next open. There are no overrides, no intraday decisions and no stop-losses.

Implementation

Straightforward to run.

Execution at the open

All orders are market-on-open, from signals on the prior close, so there is no look-ahead and nothing to monitor during the session. Most days bring one or two trades, and about a third bring none.

Trading costs

The simulation charges 5 bp per side on stocks, plus $0.005 a share on Contrarian trades, and 2 bp per side on futures. Quadrupling the stock cost to 20 bp per side takes about 1.2 points a year off the return.

Account size

Positions are published as a percentage of capital. The futures leg needs about $250,000 to track the model closely with whole Micro Nasdaq-100 (MNQ) contracts. Below that, contract rounding becomes a material source of tracking error.

Verification

A record you can audit.

Published before the open

Each day's book, with every position and every order for the open, is published before the US market opens. Nothing is published after the fact.

Timestamped

Every day is kept in the archive and committed to a public Git repository, so each file carries a timestamp that cannot be backdated without it showing.

One model, one book

Everyone sees the same portfolio. The rules are fixed and applied the same way every day.

FAQ

Common questions

How much of the record is out of sample?

The rules were designed on 2000–2021, and 2022 onward was held back. The version frozen on 13 October 2026 was tested once on 2022 to October 2026: 25.9% a year, Sharpe ratio 1.57, max drawdown −11.7%. Three changes have been made since: hard exposure limits, full-size long futures positions, and a lower volatility threshold for Core. Those were checked against the later data as well, so the current version's post-2021 figures are not a clean hold-out. The live, published record starts in November 2026.

What drives the returns?

Most of the alpha comes from Compass, a day-level market-timing signal: its returns are not explained by static factor loadings. Core behaves like a low-volatility trend book with market beta of about 0.4 to 0.5. Contrarian loads on short-term reversal. The performance page has the regressions for each strategy.

What costs and financing are assumed?

5 bp per side on stocks plus $0.005 a share on Contrarian trades, and 2 bp per side on futures. Futures are financed at the 3-month T-bill rate. Idle cash earns nothing, which understates returns when rates are high. The performance page shows returns at up to 20 bp per side on stocks and 4 bp on futures.

What about capacity?

All stock trades are in Nasdaq-100 members, no position is larger than 10% of capital, and the futures are among the most liquid contracts in the world. The simulation assumes fills at the official opening price. Market impact for large accounts has not been studied.

Is leverage used?

Stocks are always paid for in cash, never bought on margin. Net exposure is held between −100% and +100%. Gross exposure can reach 300% when Compass takes the book fully short against a full stock book, so a large gap against the futures position can cost more than the stock positions alone.

Is this investment advice?

No. It is the published output of a model, the same for everyone. It does not know your circumstances, and what you do with it is up to you.
